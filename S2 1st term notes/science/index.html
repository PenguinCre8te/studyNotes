7.1 Air¶

Components of air¶

  • Atmosphere = layer of air

  • Made up of nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide, noble gases, water vapour + other gases (from most to least)

Testing for gases in air¶

  • Burning splint goes out = Not oxygen

  • Glowing splint relights = Oxygen

Hydrogencarbonate indicator indicates the amount of carbon dioxide

lower Atmospheric higher
purple (stays) red yellow

  • Limewater turns milky = Carbon Dioxide

  • Dry cobalt chloride paper = blue

  • ->Wet cobalt chloride paper = pink -> detect water vapor

Daily applications of gases¶

  • oxygen: Help patients with breathing difficulties

  • Carbon dixoide: Used in fire extinguishers (denser then air + doesnt support burning)

  • Nitrogen: Used in food bags to prevent food from going bad

  • Noble Gases: fill light bulbs, baloons

7.2 Photosynthesis¶

  • Takes place in green plants

Process¶

word equation: Carbon dioxide + water -> Glucose + oxygen

↘ Starch (both carbondydrates)

chemical equation: $6CO_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{light energy absorbed by chlorophyll}} C_6H_{12}O_6 (\rightarrow starch)+ 6O_2$

  • The total number of each atom is same on both side

(testing for) Products of photosynthesis¶

Experiment for testing starch

  • iodine solution: turns from brown to blue black if added to starch

  • Putting leaf in boiling water -> destroys the cell membrane

  • Putting leaf in hot alchohol -> Remove chlorophyll

  • Washing leaf in hot water -> remove alchohol and make the leaf soft

  • add iodine solution -> test starch

Experiment for testing oxygen

  • Green water plant e.g. hydrilla

  • gas bubble forms after a few hours

  • the air formed relights glowing splint -> oxygen

  • Photosynthesis produces oxygen

Factors for photosynthesis¶

  • destarch: put plant in dark for 48 hours

  • -> if starch tested, the photosynthesis made it

  • Variegated leaves -> destarch -> put under light 4 hours -> test for starch:

  • part blue-black(green -> have chlorophyll->have photosynthesis) part brown(not green -> no chlorophyll->no photosynthesis)

  • Use Soda Lime to absorb Carbon dioxide -> no photosynthesis

  • Use a paper to cover part of the leaf to test for light

  • Cannot test water bc plant will die if we remove all water

  • -> Chlorophyll, Light and Carbon Dioxide and water are necessary for photosynthesis¶

Significance of photosynthesis¶

  • Animals cannot make their own food

  • Feed on plant or other animals

  • Plant = photosynthesis = maintain the balance of oxygen and carbon dioxide in nature

Food chains

  • Plant can make their own food -> producers

  • Animals cannot produce their own food -> consumers

  • Can be shown by a food chain

  • Arrow = flow of energy = pointing to the consumer

  • e.g. Phytoplankton(Producer) -> Krill(Consumer) -> Penguin(Consumer)

7.3 Respiration¶

Food = energy¶

  • Chemical energy in food -> Other forms of useful energy

Burning peanut experiment

  • Peanut can be ignited -> peanut have chemical energy $\xrightarrow {\text{Converts to}}$ heat + light energy

  • (increases water temperature)

Get energy from food with respiration¶

  • Takes place in body cells and plant cells

  • Use oxygen to break food to release energy in useful forms

  • Side product = carbon dioxide + water

$\text{Glucose} + \text{Oxygen} \xrightarrow{\text{Energy released}} \text{Carbon dioxide} + \text{Water}$

$\ce{C6H12O6 + 6O2 \xrightarrow{\text{Energy released}} 6CO2 + 6H2O}$

Photosynthesis vs Respiration¶

  • Products of photosynthesis = materials in respiration and vice versa

  • Light $\xrightarrow {\text{Photosynthesis}}$ Food (Chemical energy) $\xrightarrow{\text{Respiration}}$ Release energy

  • Respiration takes place in Plants and animals all the time

7.4 Gas exchange¶

Gas exchange in plants¶

  • Respiration and photosynthesis are gas exchages

Experiments for gas exchange in plants

  • 4 test tube covered or uncovered with or w/o leaf

  • -> plant gives out carbon dioxide in the dark and oxygen in light

Plants in daytime

  • Rate of Photosynthesis > Rate of Respiration

  • Net uptake of carbon dioxide

  • Net release of oxygen

Plants in night

  • X Photosynthesis Only respiration

  • Net uptake of oxygen

  • Net release of carbon dioxide

Stomata¶

  • Tiny pores on the leaf epidermis(surface layer of cells)

Gas exchange in amimals¶

  • Inhaled air and exhaled air

  • Collecting gas: displacement of water (a method of)

Exprement to compare inhaled and exhaled air

  • fill gas jar with exhaled air by displacing water

  • Exhaled air contain less oxygen, more carbon dioxide and more water vapour then inhaled air

  • and hotter

  • mealworm also produces carbon dioxide

Human breathing system¶

  • Air $\xrightarrow {enters}$ nasal cavity -> trachea -> bronchi -> bronchioles -> air sacs (gas exchange)

Air sacs

  • Oxygen -> air sac -> blood capillaries $\xrightarrow {\text{more oxygen}}$ body cells -> respiration $\xrightarrow {\text{more carbon dioxide}}$ blood capillaries -> out
  • Carbon doixide out of blood capillary and Oxygen go into blood capillary

Airway along the nasual cavity

  • lined with mucus-secreating cells (secretes mucus) and ciliated cells (have tiny hair on their surface)

  • tiny hairs move back and forth to sweep out the mucus in which the dust particles and bacteria are trapped

But in smokers

  • tar causes cilia to stick together and increase mucus production -> build up in airway and make airway narrower

  • mucus may block the air sacs

Harmful substance Harmful effects
Tar (brown sticky) Lung cancer, cough and respiratory diesases
Nicotine (oily liquid) Addiction, heart rate and blood pressure increase -> heart diesases
Carbon monoxide (toxic gas) Reduces oxygen content in blood (occupying red blood cells) + Heart beats faster to provide enough oxygen -> heart diseases

9 Acids and alkalis¶

Acids

  • Toliet cleaner, personal care products, drugs

  • Common acids in laboratories

  • Hydrochloric acid ($HCl$)

  • Sulphuric acid ($H_2SO_4$)

  • Nitric acid ($HNO_2$)

  • Property

  • Sour

  • Conduct electricity

  • reacts with alkali and metals

  • Uses

  • Preservatives: Acids kill microorganisms

  • Removing stains: e.g. toliet cleaner

  • Make industrial chemicals

Alkalis

  • Glass cleaners, kitchen cleaner, toothpaste, baking soda, soap, alkaline batteries

  • Laboratories

  • Sodium hydroxide ($NaOH$)

  • Ammonia solution ($NH_3 (aq)$)

  • Limewater (Calcium hydroxide) ($Ca(OH)_2$)

  • Properties

  • Removing grease

  • Making industrial chemical

  • Handling acids and alkalis safely in the laboratory

  • Read the hazard warning labels

  • Wear safety goggles, protective gloves

  • Wash hand

  • Pour into appropriate waste bottles

  • Never taste acids / alkalis

  • Do not mix acids / alkalis w/o teacher permission

  • How to handle accidents

  • Wash under water for 10 min

  • Remove any contaminated clothing and avoid making contact

  • If in eye wash with eye wash bottle for 10 min

  • Call ambulance

9.2 Ways to measure pH value¶

Acid/Alkali indicators

  • Use indicators to show presense (not pH) of acids and alkaline

Natural indicators

  • e.g. Red cabbage extract

  • Contains pigments

Litmus

  • Litmus solution / litmus paper

  • Red = acid

  • Blue = alkaline

  • acid: turn blue into red

  • alkali: turn red into blue

Universal indicators

  • Mixture of serveral indicators

  • Shows colours based on acidity or alkalinity

  • paper form = pH paper

  • Each colour corresponds to a pH value

  • Range of pH value(0-14) = pH scale

  • 7 = neutral

pH meters

  • Can measure pH value more accurately

  • Can measure the pH value of a liquid of deep colour

  • Can be reused

  • More expensive

  • larger size

  • Has to be calibrated

  • Consists of a probe connected to a digital display

pH sensors

  • connected to a data logger

  • can be recorded and analysed by a computer

Neutralization¶

  • Acids neutralizes alkalis (and vice versa) -> neutralization

  • neutralization graph: No change -> quick increase -> To the others pH value

  • Acid + Alkali -> salt + water

  • e.g. Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide = sodium chloride + water

  • Mass is conserved

Applications

  • Prevent tooth decay (alkaline toothpaste neutralise acids in mouth)

  • Relieving stomach upset (neutralize excess acids in stomachs)

  • Treating insect stings and bites (neutralize acids in bee ant and mosquito bites and alkalis in wasp stings)

  • Treating industrial waste (neutralize the waste)

  • Regulating soil pH value (add slaked lime(calcium hydroxide) to neutralize soil)

9.4 Corrosive nature of acids¶

Acid + metal

  • Acids corrode metals

  • acid + metal -> salt + hydrogen

  • e.g. Hydrochloric acid + Magnesium -> magnesium chloride + hydrogen

Acid + carbonate

  • Marble = Calcium Carbonate = Limstone

  • Acid + Carbonate -> Salt + Carbon dixoide + Water

  • e.g. Hydrochloric acid + Calcium carbonate -> Calcium chloride + Carbon dioxide + Water

Acid rain

  • Rain pH value = 5.6

  • lower = acid rain

  • Causes

  • By air pollutants e.g. sulphur dioxide / nitrogen oxides (Acidic gases)

  • Caused by bruning of fossil fuels

  • Acidic gases dissolve in water droplets to form acids -> sulpuric acid / nitric acid

  • $\xrightarrow {\text {forms}}$ Clouds $\xrightarrow {\text {fall as}}$ acid rain

  • Effects

  • Acidic gases travels by wind over long distances

  • Corroding metal objects and marble (from building materials and statues)
  • Damage plants
    • May destroy leaves of plant directly -> cannot carry out photosynthesis
    • -> cannot get enough nutrients for growth -> grow slowly or die
    • increases acidity of the soil -> plants cannot grow well
  • Harm aquatic life
    • cannot survive in water which is too acidic
    • dissolve harmful minerals -> harm

Hazards of acids and alkalis¶

Corrosive

  • Acid: Strong acid (hydrochloric acid, sulphuric acid, nitric acid), weak acid(citric acid, ethanoic acid, carbonic acid)

  • Alkali: strong alkali(potassium hydroxide, sodium hydroxide), weak aklali(ammonia solution)

  • Strong acids/alklis: Can corrode metal, skin and flesh

Handling

  • Read the precaution

  • Use in well ventilated area (vapour can irritate/corrode our respiratory tract, skin and eyes)

  • Never mix household cleaners

Diluting

  • In a well ventilated room

  • Wear safety goggles and protective gloves

  • Add TO a large amount of water

  • Stir the solution slowly as you add

  • never

  • add water to it

  • dilute with bare hands

  • inhale vapour
