- <mark>Religious movement</mark> in the 16th - 17th centuries
- Included the **split of the Church**
- **Changed European traditions**
#### Background
Christians **questioning** the Church
- Studied and emended (correcting errors) the early copies of the Bible
- Found **inaccuracies in Latin Bible**
- Church has <mark>misinterpreted</mark> some Christian **doctrines**(信條，教義，學說)
- In 1516 Dutch theologian **Erasmus** published the original Greek version of the **new testament**
- Europeans subsequently found out more misinterpretations made by the church
Rulers discontent
- Medieval
  - Church had <mark>great influence on politics</mark>

- Late Medieval (renaissance)
  
  - <mark>Strong kings</mark> emerged
  
  - <mark>discontened </mark>with the Church and wanted to <mark>regain their powers</mark>

**Wrongdoings** of the Church

- Late Medieval
  
  - Church became <mark>corrupt</mark>
  
  - ran casinos and sold '**<mark>indulgences</mark>**' 
  - (-> claims to reduce sufferings of the purchasers and their relatives in purgatory)
  
  - **Set off reformation**

#### Development of the Reformation

Immediate cause: selling of indulgences

- Church sold massive **indulgences** to raise money for building St. Peter's Basilica

- Some clergy <mark>exaggerated</mark> the function of indulgences

- Angered many German theologians

<mark>Martin Luther</mark> and the <mark>Ninety-Five Thesis</mark>

- **German priest** Martin Luther posted the 'Ninety-Five Theses' on a cathedral

- stressed that people could only be redeemed through faith in god

- criticized the corruption of the Church and priests

- Raised the issue of indulgences for discussion

- Wrote **Ninety-five thesis in Latin and translated to German** + spread throughout German

- Further translated into different languages -> strong reactions of people in Europe -> **The Reformation**

Rise of the protestant Church

- Action hindered the **revenues of indulgences** and the Church's **pristiege**

- Martin Luther was later **excommunicated** from the Church

- **German people and nobles supported him**

- -> he set up a new sect <mark>Lutheranism</mark>

- Other sects including the Calvinism and Anglican Church were established

- $\uparrow$ Known as <mark>Protestantism</mark>

- Rulers even withdrew from the Roman Catholic Church

- made Protestamtism their official religons

Counter Reformatiom

- To **restore its prestige**

- to **surpress the Protestent Church**

- Carried out **reforms**

- Rulers who still follow the church are granted **more autonomy**

- **Banned** the sales of **indulgences**

- Recognised some **societies** to promote its image

### Impact of the reformation

Conflicts and war

- Split christianity into Roman Catholic Church and the Protestant Church

- Led to **political instability**

- -> **thirty years war**: first great war in modern Europe

Changing the outlook of Europe

- Political
  
  - **Weakened** Roman Catholic Church
  
  - Rulers split religon from politics
  
  - and regained power from the Church
  
  - established **Absolute rule**

- Economic 
  
  - Protestantism, like humanism, stressed <mark>worldly life</mark>
  
  - Glorify god by <mark>working hard</mark>
  
  - **favourited the developement of Europe** (especially in protestant countries)

- Intellectual and social aspects
  
  - Protestantism advocated <mark>religious tolerance and freedom</mark>
  
  - Encouraged <mark>public meetings</mark>
  
  - showed the **ideological seeds of <mark>liberaty and democracy</mark>** in Modern Europe
  
  - Protestant church used education as a **way to preach**
  
  - Built schools and taught in relatively simple langauges
  
  - Taught **new subjects** e.g. natural science
  
  - Became more **popular** in Europe and education standard of Europeans improved
