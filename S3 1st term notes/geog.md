1.1 Major natural hazards

- Natural event

  - Caused by natural processes or forces out of human control

- Natural hazards

  - Natural event that cause harm and loss to humans

- Natural desasters

  - Great loss of life and severe property damage

- Major natural hazards

  - Geological

    - Volcanic eruption

    - Earthquake

    - Landslide

  - Meteorological hazard

    - Drought

    - Flood

    - Typhoon

  - Biological hazard

    - Pest (e.g. Plague of locusts)

1.2 Location of natural hazards

- Natural hazards caused by processes or forces occurring in different environments

- -> distribution is related to the location of the environments where they form

- e.g. Floods are near rivers and coastal areas

- e.g. Typhoon mainly found in the tropical regions

- Few places are free of natural hazards

### Landslides

3.1 What

- Collapse of slopes due to gravity

- Natural terrain

3.2 Effects

- Cause injuries and deaths

- Bury buildings and damage infrastructure

- Block roads and stop traffic

3.3 Why

- Driving force

  - Pulls slope materials downhill under gravity

  - Slope gradient

  - Weight of slope materials

- Resisting force

  - Prevent slope materials from sliding down

  - Cohesion of slope materials

  - Friction

- Driving force > Resisting force => Unstable and may collapse

**3.4 Causes**

Natural Factors

- Heavy rainfall

  - Heavy rainfall -> Rainwater adds weight to the slope -> More likely to fall

  - Rainwater -> Reduce cohesion and friction -> Resisting force decreases

- Weathering

  - Granite is hard when fresh but can easily be weathered under hot and wet climate

  - -> Deeply weathered granite

  - -> Thick layer of loose material on slopes -> Small resisting force

- Large slope gradoent

  - 60% has > $$20\degree$$ slope gradient

  - Larger slope gradient -> Stronger the force pulling slope material downhill

Human factors

- Many landslide occur near built-up areas

- Cut and filled slopes

  - -> Build road and buildings

  - -> Increases slope gradient -> Driving force

  - -> Removed vegetation -> Resisting force

  - Some slopes are developed without adequate preventive measures -> landslides

  - Stricter laws and better slope management -> Safe

- Poor maintenance of man-made slope

  - Cracked slope surface and drainage channels -> More water to seep into soil

  - -> Increases driving force, reduces resisting force

**3.5 How to prepare for landslides**

Preventive measures

- Building barriers    

  - Flexible barrier

  - Check dam

  - Reduce the loss of life and property

- Strengthening and monitoring slopes

  - -> Reduce driving force, increasing resisting force

  - Retaining wall -> support a steep slope

  - Soil nails -> anchor the soil to the slope

  - Gabion wall -> provide support at the foot of the slope

  - Covering slope with shotcrete -> Prevent water from seeping in

  - Weepholes -> drain water

  - Drainage channels -> drain water from slope surface

- Setting up laws

  - Restrict development on steep slope

  - Require slope owners to carry out proper slope maintenance measures

- Setting up warning systems

  - Heavy rain -> Landslip warning

  - -> Reduce exposure to potential landslide and stay away from slopes

- Education

  - When landslip warning

    - Avoid near steep slopes

    - Avoid driving on roads with landslip warning signposts

    - Stay at home or in a safe place

    - Move to a safe shelter

Remedial measures

- Well-trained rescue teams and emergency aid

- Temporary shelters

- Financial aid and insurance compensation -> rebuild homes
