Safety rules¶

Safety rules e.g.

  • Do not work without supervision of teacher

  • Follow strictly the instructions given by the teacher

  • Clean up all spillages at once

  • Clean up the bench after experiemnts

  • Understand the hazards

  • Wear eye protection

  • Turn off Bunsen burner immediately or turn to a small luminous flame

  • Be careful of shooting out of hot liquids during heating

  • Handle corrosive liquids only with tongs

  • Pour acid into a large amount of water with constant stirring

  • Do not eat or drink

  • Wash hands before leaving

Hazard Warning Labels

  • Explosive (e.g. potassium / sodium turned yellow)

  • Oxidizing (e.g. Concentrated sulpuric / nitric acid)

  • Flammable (e.g. alcohol, hydrogen)

  • Carcinogenic (Cause cancer, e.g. Benzene, tetrachloromethane)

  • Toxic (e.g. Chlorine, Bromine, cyanides)

  • Harmful (e.g. iodine)

  • Corrosive (e.g. concentrated mineral acids)

  • Irritant (e.g. Dilute mineral acids)

  • Orders

  • Flammable < oxidizing < explosive

  • Harmful < toxic

  • Irritant < corrosive

Storage of Chemicals

  • Prevent contact of air -> Under paraffin oil

  • Mercury covered with paraffin oil -> Prevent evaporation (vapour highly toxic)

  • Flammable / Volatile -> Stored in a cool place away from direct sunlight or heat sources

  • Absorb moisture (e.g. NaOH) -> well sealed containers

  • Decompose in presence of light -> Brown bottles

  • Sodium hydroxide -> Rubber stopper (glass stopper stucks in the bottle neck)

Laboratory techniques¶

Handling of chemicals

  • Transferring solids: With spatula

  • Transferring liquids

  • From reagent bottle to test tube: direct pour

  • From beaker: via a glass rod

  • Mixing substances

  • Tapping

  • Stoppered and shake up and down

  • Pouring from test tube to another: direct pour

Heating

  • Bunsen burner

  • Lighting a Bunsen burner

    1. Close the air hole

    2. Hold a lighted match near the top

    3. Turn on the gas tap

    4. Close the air hole (-> non-luminous)

. Struck back

. Gas supply turned down too much with air hole open

. -> Turn of gas supply

. Wrap hand with cloth and close the air hole the light again

  • Heating liquid in test tube

  • Hold with a test tube holder near the top and swirl it continuously (-> Heating is uniform)

  • Wear eye protection

  • Do not fill a tube to $>\frac 1 3$ capacity

  • Do not point the tube to anybody

  • Heating solid in test tube

  • Test tube slightly sloped downward

  • -> prevent condensed liquid formed from flowing back to the hot bottom of test tube and cracking it

  • Water bath: put test tube in beaker with water, for low-boiling point / flammable liquids

  • Oil bath: higher temperature can be reached

  • Steam bath: Evaporate solution quickily (e.g. obtaining crystals)

Collection of Gases

  • Displacement of water

  • -> Gases insoluble in water

  • Cannot be used when gases are required dry

  • Downward delivery

  • Soluble gases denser than air

  • Upward delivery

  • Soluble gases less dense than air

  • Gas syringe

  • Used for any gas

  • e.g. connected to a conical flask

  • Smelling gases

  • Hold the test tube 10cm away

  • Fan a little gas towards your nose

Separating mixtures¶

  • Separate into pure state before they become useful

  • Solutes dissolves in solvent -> Solution

  • Dilute solution, concentrated solution: relatively small / large amount of solute in a given amount of solvent

  • saturated solution: Dissolved the maximum amount of solute it can

Methods of separation

  • Solid + liquid

  • insoluble: Filtration

    • -> Residue (remain on filter paper) + filtrate (collects in beaker)

  • dissolved:

    • Evaporation: -> Increases concentration

    • Crystalization

  • Liquid + liquid

  • Solvent and solute from solution: Distillation

  • Two miscible liquids: Fractional distillation

  • Two immiscible liquids: Separating funnel

Crystalization

  • By cooling hot concentrated solution

  • Water is boiled away before

  • Crystals formed on glass rod on cooling -> Saturated

  • Concentrated solution cools -> Crystals

  • By evaporating solution at room temperature

  • Solvent evaporate -> concentrated and saturated -> Crystalization

  • By slow evaporation / cooling -> Large and well formed

  • Can be seperated by filtration, taken out with spatula and dried by pressing between filter papers

Simple distillation

  • Separate liquid and non-volatile (not easily evaporated) solid

  • Hot vapour formed condense back to liquid on cold surface -> distillate

  • Add anti-bumping granules to prevent pumping due to overheating (may splash out)

  • Water should be passed from the bottom for a better cooling effect

Fractional distillation

  • To separate miscible liquids that boiling points are not too close
  • Fractionating column is packed with glass beads
  • -> Larger surface area for condensation and vaporzation
  • e.g. Separating ethanol and water
  • Vapour contains more ethanol because ethanol is more volatile
  • Vapour condenses when it touches the cold glass beads in the fractionating column
  • Continuous rise of hot vapour -> condensed vapour boils again
  • As vapour rises, it contains more and more ethanol
  • Vapour escapes consists of almost pure ethanol
  • Temperature is measured by a thermometer
  • Remains steady then rises quickily then remains steady: At first the escaping vapour contains almost pure ethanol -> Recorded boiling point of ethanol
  • Different in boiling points should $>10\degree C$

Separating funnel

  • to separate immiscible liquids

  • Mixture place in, shaken and allowed to settle

  • -> Two layers, less dense one on top

  • Remove stopper, open tap, and two liquid will run out in turns and collected in separate containers
