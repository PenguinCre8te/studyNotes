Introduction¶

Characteristics of organisms

  • Movement

  • Respiration

  • Sensitivity

  • Growth

  • Reproduction

  • Excretion -> Homeostasis

  • Nutrition

Cells

  • Robert Hooke observed cell walls of dead cork cells

  • Theodor Schwann proposed the Cell Theory

  • All organisms are made up of one or more cells

  • Cell is the basic unit of life

  • All cells come from pre-existing cells (cell division)

Microscopes

  • Light microscopes

  • Light passes through specimen (can see some internel structurs)

  • Max 1600x

  • Coloured image

  • Living specimens can be observed, prepared easily

  • Transmission electron microscopes

  • Electron beam pass through a very thin slice of a specimen

  • 150 000 000x

  • Black and White two-dimensional showing internal structures

  • Scanning electron microscopes

  • Electron beam scan over the surface

  • 200 000x

  • Black and white three-dimensional showing ONLY the external structures

  • Magnification and resolution (clearer, more details) is higher

  • Cannot observe live specimen

Light microscope

  • Compound microscope: eyepiece and objective

  • Eyepiece: magnifying lens

  • Body tube

  • Nosepiece: Choose the objective required

  • Objective: Magnifying lens pointing to the specimen

  • Condenser: Focus light onto specimen

  • Diaphragm: Adjust amount of light

  • Light source

  • Arm: Carry microscope

  • Coarse adjustment knob: Get rough focus

  • Fine adjustment knob: Get sharp focus

  • Stage

  • Base: supports microscope

  • Light penetrates the specimen, enters the objective, passes through eyepiece and enters our eye

  • Image is inverted

Magnification

  • $\text {Total magnification} = \text {Magnification of eyepiece} \times \text {Magnification of objective}$

  • Low-power magnification: Larger area, Fewer details, Brighter

  • High-power magnification: Smaller area, More details, Dimmer

Scientific method

  1. Making observations

  2. Asking a question

  3. Proposing a hypothesis (Not needed when you do not need explanation)

  4. Making a prediction

  5. Testing the hypothesis (e.g. experiments)

  6. Drawing a conclusion

Variables

  • Independent variable (to be investigated)

  • Dependent variable (to be measured)

  • Control variable (to be kept the same)

Improving accuracy: Repeat experiment

Carbohydrates

  • Made up of Carbon (C), Hydrogen (H), Oxygen (O) atoms

  • H:O ratio = 2:1

  • Monosaccharide

  • Simplest form of carbohydrate

  • Glucose, Fructose: Fruits and honey

  • Galactose: Milk and diary products

  • Disaccharides

  • 2 x Monosaccharide molecules $$ \xrightleftharpoons[\text{Hydrolysis}]{\text{Condensation}}$$ Disaccharide

  • Maltose: 2 x glucose (Germinating barley)

  • Sucrose: glucose + fructose (Sugar cane, sugar beet, table sugar)

  • Lactose: glucose + galactose (Milk and diary products)

  • Polysaccharides

  • Condensation of many monosaccharide

  • Do not taste sweet, insoluble in water

  • Starch: Storage form in plant

  • Glycogen: Storage form in animals

  • Cellulose: Component of plant cell walls (A source of dietary fibre -> cannot be digested)

  • Functions

  • Energy source

  • Starch converted into glucose -> respiration to release energy

  • Converted into glycogen (stored in liver and skeletal muscles) / lipids -> stored as energy reserves

  • As a source of dietary fibre

Lipids

  • Fats Oils
    Solid Liquid (at room temp)
    Mainly animals Mainly plants
    Butter, lard Peanut oil, corn oil

  • Made up of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen atoms

  • H:O ratio >> 2:1 (e.g. 10:1)

  • Insoluble in water (soluble in organic solvents)

  • e.g. Triglycerides (1 x glycerol + 3 x fatty acids $$ \xrightleftharpoons[\text{Hydrolysis}]{\text{Condensation}}$$ Triglyceride + 3 $H_2O$)

  • Classified into fats and oils

  • Functions

  • Energy reserves: Stored in adipose tissues under the skin as subcutaneous fat or around internal organs

  • -> Shock absorber to protect internal organs

  • Heat insulator (subcutaneous fat): Reduces heat loss from body

  • Transportation, absorption and storage of lipid-soluble vitamins

  • Produce some hormones

  • Component of cell membranes
