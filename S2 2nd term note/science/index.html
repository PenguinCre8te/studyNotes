Electricity¶

(what is) Electricity¶

  • Very important

  • Have path for electricity to flow -> electric circuit

  • Components

  • Source of electricity + electrical energy

  • Device using electrical energy

  • Wires connecting

  • Conditions

  • closed circuit

  • source of electrical energy

  • Types of material

  • electrical insulators

  • electrical conductors

  • Switch

  • open or close a circuit

Circuit diagrams

  • Circuit symbols

  • Circuit diagram

  • represent a circuit

  • Symbols

  • Electric cell

Current¶

  • Atom

  • Nucleus surrounded by electrons

  • nucleus = positive

  • electron = negative

  • Some electrons can escape and move freely -> free electrons

  • Conventional current: from positive pole to negative pole

  • Flow of free electrons: from negative pole to positive pole

Electric current

  • measure with ammeter

  • unit: (A) ampere

  • should be connected in series

Effects of electric current

  • Heating effect

  • Magnetic effect

  • -> electromagnet

    • Can be switched on or off
Voltage¶

  • Energy supplied to free electrons

  • Measure: voltmeter

  • Unit: (V) volt

  • Should be connected in parallel

  • Voltage across = sum of all the electric cells

  • Voltage is directly proportional to the current

Resistance¶

  • opposition of material to the flow of electric current

  • unit: ohm ($\Omega$)

  • Some higher some lower

  • Factors

  • Material

  • Thickness

  • Length

  • Resistor

  • Fixed value of resistance

  • Rheostat

  • Changeable resistance

  • Sliding rheostat

  • rotary-type rheostat

  • Application

    • Dimmer switch

    • Hi-fi volume control

Series and parallel circuits

  • Series circuit -> connected in series
  • Main current = Sum of branches current
  • Voltage same
  • Parallel circuit -> connected in parallel
  • Same current
  • Different voltage

How do they work

Mains electricity¶
  • Mains electricity
  • Mains voltage = 220V

Connecting

  • Plug into a mains socket
  • Three-pin plug
  • live pin
  • neutral pin
  • earth pin
  • Mains socket

  • Earth hole

  • Neutral hole (Left)

  • Live hole (Right)

  • Wires
  • Live wire
  • Earth wire
  • Neutral wire
  • only live and neutral deliver power

Domestic circuits

  • Power plant -> Consumer unit -> Appliances (in parallel)
  • -> one appliance turned off other can still work
Electric safety with household electricity¶
  • 700 fire each year caused by electrical accidents
  • Circuit breaker protects us

Overloading

  • Connect too much electrical appliances -> overloading
  • Very hot -> fire/explosion

Short circuit

  • Low resistance path -> Short circuit
  • Wire are loose or worn out
  • With dust built up on it
  • in faulty electronic devices such as damaged phones

Safety devices

  • Fuses
  • Breaking the circuit when current is large
  • Wire in fuse become hot and melts -> 'the fuse blows'
  • Prevent overheating
  • Fuse has Fuse rating
  • Use a fuse with fuse rating slightly higher than the circuit under normal operation
  • Circuit breakers
  • in Consumer units
  • Exceeds rating -> Circuit breaker switch off
  • need not be replaced
  • Earth wire
  • Connect metal casing of electrical appliance to ground -> earthing
  • Do not have a ground wire -> electric shock
  • Instead flow through ground and not us

Safety precautions

  • Use appliances with three-pin plug with earth wire
  • Do not overload mains sockets
  • Do not use electrical appliances in wet places
  • Do not use damaged plugs and universal adaptors
  • Never pull on a cable to remove
  • Do not place cable near heater or stove
Cost of electricity and power¶

Power

  • energy per time
  • watt
  • kilowatt
  • $\text{power} (W) = \frac {\text {energy}(J)} {\text{time}(s)}$

Cost

  • Measurement
  • Joulemeter: Use in laboratory
    • Kilowatt-hour meter: Used by electricity companies: kW h (one unit of electricity on electric bill)
  • Joules <=> kW h
  • $1kWh = \text{3 600 000} J$
  • $\text{electrical energy} (kW\text{ } h) = \text{power } (kW) \times \text{time }(h)$
  • Cost
  • $Cost = \text{units of electricity used} \times \text{costs per unit}$

Efficiency

  • $\text{efficiency} = \frac {\text{useful power output}} {\text{power input}}\times 100\%$
  • LED: efficient
  • filament bulb: inefficient

10 Sensing the environment¶

10.1 Senses and sense organs¶

Sensitivity

  • ability to detect and respond to stimuli (Changes in environment that causes a respond)
  • Important

Sense organs

  • Body parts that detect stimuli

  • Contain specialised sensory cells for detecting stimuli

  • -> Send signals through nerves -> brain -> produces sense

  • Stimuli Sense organ Sense produced
    light eye sight
    sound ear hearing
    smells (of chemical substances) tongue taste
    flavours (of chemical substances) nose smell
    touch, pressure, pain, temperature skin touch
10.2 Sight¶

Main parts of eye

  • Eyebrow: Prevent sweat from getting into

  • Eyelash: Prevent small particles from getting into the eyes

  • Eyelid

  • Close to protect the eyes (from dirt and strong light)

  • Blink to help moisten eye surface

  • Hidden inside socket of the skull: protect eyeball

Parts of eye

  • Focusing muscle: Change thickness of the lens

  • Iris: Control size of the pupil

  • Pupil: Regulate the amount of light entering the eye

  • Cornea: Let light pass through, helps focus light

  • Lens: Let light pass through, helps focus light

  • Jelly-like fluid: Maintain shape of the eyeball, help focus light

  • Sclera: protects the eyeball

  • Retina: Contain light-sensitive cells -> Detect light -> Produce signals

  • Blind spot

  • Where optic nerve leaves eyeball

  • Contain no light-sensitive cells

  • Optic nerve: Light-sensitive cells -> signals -> transmit via optic nerve -> brain

How is image formed

  • Light -> Cornea -> Lens ->** Jelly like fluid** -> A sharp and inverted image formed on retina

  • . \____ Help focus the light ____/

  • -> light-sensitive cells stimulated by lights -> Produce signals -> optic nerve -> brain

  • -> Interprets the signals as a upright image

Focusing

  • Near

  • Focusing muscles contract

  • Lens become thicker

  • Distant

  • Focusing muscles relax

  • Lens become thinner

Colours

  • Cone cells: Colour, works in bright light (three types: sensitive to red, green, or blue)

  • Rod cells: Cannot detect colour, works in dim light

Limitations¶

  • Cannot see things too small or too far away -> use optical instruments

  • e.g. Binoculars, Hand lens, (Light, Electron) microscope, Astronomical telescope

Blind spot

  • On retina where optic nerve leaves the eyeball
Eye defects¶

Short sight

  • Image of far object formed in front of retina

  • Lens being too thick / eyeball being too long

  • Corrected by wearing glasses with concave lens

Long sight

  • Image of near object formed behind retina

  • Lens being too thin / eyeball being too short

  • Corrected by wearing glasses with convex glasses

Astigmatism

  • Cornea or lens is unevenly curved

  • -> Light cannot be focused on retina to form a sharp image

  • Corrected by wearing glasses with lens that have different curvature at different points

Cataract

  • Lens become cloudy

  • Can lead to blindness

  • Causes: old age, eye injuries, prolonged exposure to sunlight, heredity

  • Lens can be replaced with artificial lens

Colour-blindness

  • Defect in one or more types of cone cells -> affect ability to distinguish colours

  • e.g. Red-green colour-blindness -> difficulty in distinguishing red and green

  • Inherited defect, More common in males than in females

  • Cannot be corrected

Protecting our eyes

  • Rest every 30-40min

  • Keep 30cm from book 50-70 from computer, read in sufficient light

  • Wear safety googles

  • Have regular eye checkups, correct eye defects as soon as possible

  • Wear hat and sunglasses when strong sunlight

10.3 Hearing¶
Sound¶

  • Produced by vibration of object -> Push surrounding air particles -> vibrate -> travel through air

  • Number of vibration per second = frequency (unit: Hertz Hz)

  • Must travel through a medium (solid, liquid or gas)

  • Through vibration of particles in those media

  • Cannot travel through vacuum

Hearing¶

  • Pinnia (funnel shape) -> Ear canal                                                                                                     } Outer ear

  • -> Eardrum (thin membrane) vibrate -> Ear bones (amplify vibrations)                             } Middle ear

  • -> Cochlea (coiled structure) -> Sensory hair cells (detect vibration and produce signals) } Inner ear

  • -> auditory nerve -> interpreted as sound                                                                                 }

Limitations¶

Audible frequency range

  • Can only hear sounds from 20Hz to 20 000Hz

  • Younger -> Higher upper image

  • Other animals

  • Different animals have different audible frequency ranges

  • e.g. Dolphin 1000 - 100 000

Noise pollution¶

Noise

  • Sound that is unwanted and disturbing

  • e.g. Traffic, construction work, human voices, loud music

  • -> Noise pollution

  • Unit: Decibel (dB)

  • Use decibel meter to measure

Effect of Noise

  • 65dB: Work and sleep disturbance

  • 80dB: Hearing damage

  • 110dB: Hearing loss after 15 min exposure

  • 130dB: Ear painful

Protect hearing

  • Important

  • Ways to protect hearing

  • Keep music volume low

  • Shorten time staying in noisy environment

  • Wear ear plugs or ear protectors

  • Do not pick ear with hard objects

10.4 Smell and taste¶
Smell¶

  • Smell receptors in nose

  • Can detect chemicals in air when breathed in

  • Chemical from food or substances spread in air

  • -> Breathe in -> Enter our nose and dissolve in mucus in nasal cavity

  • -> Stimulate smell receptors -> signals

  • -> sent through nerves -> Brain -> interpreted as smell

Taste¶

  • Small projections on it

  • Contain taste buds

    • Contain taste receptors

  • Chemicals in food dissolve in saliva -> stimulate taste receptors -> signals

  • -> sent through nerves -> Brain -> interpreted as taste

Relationship¶

  • Smell affects sense of taste

  • Use both to determine the full flavour of the food

  • Blocked nose -> Sense of smell weaker -> Can only use tongue to detect flavour -> food tastes bland

10.5 Touch¶

Skin

  • Largest organ

  • Contain Touch receptor, temperature receptor, pressure receptor and pain receptor

  • Stimulated -> send signals to the brain through nerves -> interprets and produces senses we experience

Sensitivity to touch

  • Receptors are not evenly distributed

  • Fingertips have a large number of touch receptors -> very sensitive to touch

  • -> Blind people read Braille with fingertips

Sensitivity to temperature

  • Not reliable in detecting hot and cold
10.6 Sense of balance¶

  • Eyes: information about position based on what we see around us

  • Muscles and joints: movement and position of body

  • Semi-circular canals

  • Contain sensory hair cells surrounded by a gel-like structure and a fluid

  • 3 of them lies on a different plane at right angles to each other -> detect movements of head in different directions

  • Head moves -> fluid moves and stimulates the sensory hair cells

  • Send signals through nerves to the brain -> interprets it to determine the position and movements of the head

  • -> Interprets the signals to produce sense of balance

10.7 Brain and our senses¶

Role of our brain

  • Sense organs: responsible for detecting stimuli and sending signals to the brain

  • brain: integrates and interprets the signals -> produce senses

  • Decides on what actions to take -> send signals to the muscles through nerves -> response

Reaction time

  • -> time between detecting a stimulus and making a response

  • Takes time for brain to receive signals from the sense organs, interpret the signals then make a response

Parts of the brain

  • Cerebrum

  • Integrates and interprets the signals from sense organs

  • Send signals to muscles to make response

  • Responsible for intelligence, memory and thinking

  • Medulla

  • Controls basic life functions (e.g. Heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure)

  • Cerebellum

  • Coordinates muscles to produce body movements

    • Maintains body balance

Illusions

  • Brain interprets signals incorrectly -> illusion
Effect of alcohol, drugs and organic solvents on senses¶

  • Coordination (between sense organs, brain and muscles) are important

  • Some substances (e.g. alcohol, drugs, organic solvents) can affect this coordination

  • Abusing can have harmful effects

Drinking alcohol

  • e.g. Beer, wine, spirits

  • Affects

  • Slow down our reaction to stimuli (longer reaction time)

  • Leads to blurred vision and unclear speech

  • Long term drinking can damage brain and liver

  • Affects judgement and memory

  • Causes addiction

  • Causes difficulty with body coordination

Taking drugs

  • Chemical substances that can affect our body functions

  • Use drugs in a incorrect way for non-medical purposes -> drug abuse

  • Some people take illegal drugs -> extremely harmful: Damages organs, may lead to deaths

  • e.g. Cocane (coke), Cannabis (weed), Methylamphetamine (ice), heroin, MDMA (ecatacy), Ketamine

  • Harms

  • Blurred vision

  • Loss of appetite -> malnutrition

  • Permanent damage to organs

  • Brain

    • Addiction

    • hallucination

    • poor judgement and memory

    • slow reaction

    • sleep problems

  • Skin sores

  • Uncontrolled shaking of head and limbs

  • Frequent and painful urination

  • Unable to control urination

  • Reasons

  • Wrongly believe can get relief from boredom and stress

  • Because of peer pressure

  • Bad

inhaling organic solvents

  • e.g. thinner, glue, correction pen

  • by inhaling the vapour

  • Reach the brain -> affect senses and judgement

  • May become excited and violent -> hurt themselves and others

  • Permanently damage many organs, may even lead to death

11.1 Motion¶

Speed

  • How fast object moves

  • distance per unit time

  • Unit: ms⁻¹ or km h⁻¹

  • e.g. walking speed about 1.1 ms⁻¹ or 4 km h⁻¹

Average speed ($ms^{-1}$)

  • = $\frac {\text{distance}(m)} {\text{time}(s)}$

Distance time graph

  • Represent motion of a object

  • Format

  • Title: Distance-time graph of object

  • y-axis: distance(unit)

  • x-axis: time(unit)

  • Rest: horizontal line

  • Higher the speed higher the slope

Uniform motion vs non uniform motion

  • Uniform motion

  • Moving at a constant speed in a fixed direction

  • e.g. People on escalator

  • Non-uniform motion

  • Changing speed or changing direction

  • e.g. A car is breaking
