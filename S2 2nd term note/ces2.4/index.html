1 Economic performance¶

1.1 Economic performance indicators¶

  • Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

  • Total value of production (total value of products and services)

  • Faster growth = Better economic performance

  • Per capita Gross Domestic product (Per capita GDP)

  • $= \frac {\text{GDP}} {\text{Total population}}$

  • Higher = Higher average income of people

  • -> Economic prosperity

  • Unemployment rate

  • $\frac {\text{Unemployed population}} {\text{Labour force}} \times 100 \%$

  • Labour force: all working and employed population aged 15 or above

  • Worse economic situation -> fewer people hired -> unemployment rate increase

  • General price level

  • Price of items generally traded in the market

  • Increase = inflation

  • Decrease = deflation

  • Consumer Price Index (CPI)

  • Price level of goods generally bought by households

  • CPI rises -> Increase in general price level and cost of living

  • % Change = Inflation rate / Deflation rate

  • Composite Consumer Price Index (CCPI)

  • Based on overall expenditure pattern of all household taken together -> Impact of consumer price change as a whole

1.2 Industrial structures¶

  • Primary production: Extract natural resources (e.g. Agriculture)

  • Secondary production

  • Processing raw materials into products (semi-finished or finished)

  • e.g. Clothing industry

  • Tertiary production: Provide services (e.g. Retail, Tourism)

  • 90% of GDP from Tertiary in HK since 2005, continued to rise

  • 88% Employed in Tertiary industry

Change of economic performance indicators

  • GDP: Generally increased

  • Unemployment rate: fluctuated but remained low

  • GDP increase -> Higher demand for labour -> Unemployment rate decrease

Events that caused changes

  • 1997-1998: Asian financial crises -> Stock and real estate hit, Reduced willingness to consume

  • 1999-2001: Slightly recovered, 9/11 Caused recession in global economy

  • Early 2003: SARS reduced tourism, investments and consumption

  • June 2003: CPG introduced

  • Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement

    • -> Promote trade, Support Hong Kong's economic development

  • Individual visit scheme

    • Mainland tourists to travel to Hong Kong

  • 2008: Affected by global financial crisis -> decline in GDP and increase in unemployment rate

  • 2010-2018: Economic growth resumed

  • 2019: Global economic growth softened, China-US trade conflicts, Local social incidents -> Negative growth

  • 2020: Affected by COVID-19 pandemic -> largest annual negative growth

  • 2023: relaxed COVID-19 control measures, issued consumption vouchers -> Positive Growth

1.3 Hong Kong being highly externally oriented and open¶

Externally oriented

  • Total external trade accounts for large % of GDP -> Highly externally oriented

  • External trade: Trade between a region and other countries or regions

  • Total foreign trade: Total value of imports and exports of goods and services

Open economy

  • Allow foreign residents to conduct a variety of economic activities locally

  • Local residents can freely invest elsewhere

  • Hong Kong

  • No foreign exchange controls

  • Capitals and talents can flow in and out freely

  • -> Highly open economy

  • Inward and outward direct investment increased repeatedly (2000-2003)

  • -> Has frequent cross-border capital flows and investment activities

Hong Kong economy <-> global events

  • Attract external individuals and enterprises to invest and trade

  • Heavily reliant on external trade and investment

  • -> Easily affected by the economic ups and downs of the world

Hong Kong <-> Mainland¶

Stabilising Hong Kong economy for national economic security

  • economic security: A country's ability to eliminate and resolve potential risks, resist external impacts -> stable economic development

  • Hong Kong: International financial centre and global commercial hub -> Ensure the stability and security of its business environment

  • -> safeguard national security

  • e.g. Asian financial crisis, speculators attacked HK linked exchange system, if not protect might have spread and hindered national development

Economic development of HK after 2nd world war

  • Entrepôt (轉口港)

  • Britain made Hong Kong a free port -> More trading activities

  • After second world war -> need reconstruction -> increased international trade

  • Promote development of Hong Kong's entrepôt trade

  • Establishment of PRC -> main entrepôt for trade between mainland and foreign countries

  • Industrial centre

  • Civil war in China -> Mainland resident moved to HK -> Capital, technology and management experience -> set up manufacturing factories

  • Korean war broke out: China sent army to resist US -> economically sanctioned China -> decline in entrepôt trade

  • Forced HK to undergo economic transformation to develop manufacturing industry

  • 1950s Light industries (e.g. textile, toy) -> 1960s More light industries (clocks, electronics) -> 1970s Asia's light industrial manufacturing centre

  • Cross border industrial manufacturing

  • Production cost increased since 1970s

  • Production costs in Thailand and Malaysia relatively lower

  • -> Lost the advantage of low production costs

  • Reform

    • Carry out internal economic reform

    • changing outdated economic system, production methods, management models, ideological concepts '

    • -> to lead country towards modernisation

  • Opening up

    • Open up economy externally

    • develop economic cooperation relationship with other countries

    • Attract foreign investment in China -> learn from world advance technology, equipment, and management experience

  • Hong Kong: Increased cost, less competitiveness

  • Mainland: Relaxed regulations on investment, introduced favourable measures (e.g. tax concessions, cheap land)

  • -> adopted the front shop and back factory model (moved production process to Pearl River Delta Region)

  • -> Make use of cheap land to reduce cost

  • HK use advantage in global trade to take on overseas orders, develop new products, ... -> Cross border production

  • Becoming a international trade centre

  • Foreign trade developed rapidly

  • Growth in

    • Import trade (small market -> most products exported)

    • Export trade (rely on imported raw materials and consumer goods)

    • Re-export trade (Mainland developing -> use HK as a entrepôt, has been increasing)

  • Becoming a international financial centre

  • Reform and opening-up -> demand for financial and other professional services from mainland enterprises

  • Bought opportunities to HK service industry

  • Lifted restrictions on financial market to attract foreign investment and financial institute

  • -> shifted from manufacturing to service industries (finance, accounting, trade, logistics)

  • -> Became an international financial, transportation, and trade centre

  • Country supported with 14th 5 year plan

  • -> Develop

    • Four traditional areas

    • Financial, trade, transportation centre

    • centre for international legal and dispute resolution service in the Asia pacific region

    • Four emerging areas

    • International aviation, innovation and technology hub

    • East-meet-west centre for international cultural exchange

    • Regional intellectual property trading centre

Seized opportunities

  • Mainland increasingly open to the world

  • Benefited from country's economic development

  • became a international financial, transportation, trade centre

  • Contributions to country's development

  • Introducing capital through investing in mainland

  • Introducing talents

  • Providing professional services (e.g. legal services, financial services, accounting, building surveying)

  • Serving as the bridge between the country and the world

  • Promoting investment in infrastructure (e.g. Guangzhou-Shenzhen Expressway, Humen Bridge, Shajiao Power Plant)

  • Promoting scientific research development

    • Cooperate to develop scientific research

    • Promote development of high value-added industries in the Pearl River Delta to increase regional competitiveness

  • Serving as a financing platform

    • Largest overseas fund-raising centre of the Mainland

    • Raise fund through bank loans or bonds in Hong Kong

Make good use of own advantages

  • Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area

  • Bay area consisting Hong Kong + Macau + 9 municipalities

  • One of the four core city in GBA -> drive near cities

  • Belt and road initiatives

  • Proposed by President Xi in 2013

  • Promote policy coordination along the routes

  • Strengthen flow of capital, commodities, and services across border

  • Belt (Silk road economic belt): Xi' an to Central Asia, West Asia and to Europe

  • Road (Maritime silk road): Coastal ports via south China Sea to Indian Ocean and South Pacific Ocean

  • HK has high quality professional services and international networks

  • -> make contributions to countries (in areas such as financial, insurance, construction...)

Close economic ties between the Mainland and Hong Kong

  • Seized the opportunity of the reform and opening up

  • Flow of goods, services, capital between increased significantly

  • Trade ties

  • Mainland is the largest trading partner since 1985

  • Commodity trade: Largest export market, largest supplier for imported products

  • Trade service: Largest market for Hong Kong's export and import of services

  • Hong Kong is the third largest trading partner of the Mainland

  • Total trade = 309.7B USD

  • Mainland's second largest export market

  • Investment ties

  • Hong Kong and the Mainland are each other's primary destination for outward direct investment (in 2023)

2 Economic globalisation¶

2.1 Definition¶

  • Goods and factors of production (e.g. Capital, labour, information, technology) flows freely around the world

  • -> Economic activities (e.g. Consumption, trade, investment) to transcend national boundaries and time constrains

  • -> Closer economic ties of countries around the world

Features

  • International trade liberalisation

  • Increased labour mobility

  • Commodity market integration

  • Close connection between financial services in different places

  • Globalisation of production processes

2.2 Factors¶

Transport development

  • New transported methods were introduced one after another

  • Kept developing and improving over time

  • -> Transport speed increased continuously

  • -> Reduced time and cost of cross-border transport

  • Increased cross-border movement of people and goods between countries

  • Promote economic trade, enables workers to travel easily to jobs around the world

  • Enterprises can produce, invest, sell products in different regions

  • -> Economic ties between different countries become closer

Information technology development

  • Allows information to be transmitted to different part of the world faster and at lower cost

  • Through telephone, email, video conferencing

  • -> enable enterprises to operate and sell globally

Multinational corporations

  • Corporation operating business in two or more countries

  • Set up production lines and subsidiaries to expand business in different countries and regions

  • -> develop a sale network at global scale

  • -> Substantial impact on market integration

  • Example: Starbucks

  • Management in Seattle, US

  • Procurement and production from >30 countries

  • Sales in more than 32 000 chain stores

  • ->

    • Developing a cross-regional management Model

    • Carry out their transnational economic activities by coordinating their overseas subsidiaries through headquarters

    • Outsourcing production to other countries with lower costs

    • Making a global market: Conducting operations across the world to develop investment, production and sales networks

International economic organisations

  • Established by two or more national governments

  • By concluding or joining international treaty or agreement in order to achieve common economic goals

  • Classified into global organisation and regional organisation according to scale

  • e.g.

  • World Bank

    • Promote long-term economic development and poverty alleviation

    • Provide technology and capital to country to support reform implementation or projects

    • e.g. Building schools, providing water and electricity, fighting diseases and protecting the environment

  • World trade organisation

    • Eliminates trade barriers and discriminatory measures

    • Provides mechanism to resolve trade disputes

    • Strive to promote free trade and fair competition

  • International Monetary Fund (IMF)

    • Supports economic policies that contributes to financial stability, trade development and monetary cooperation

    • Provides policy advice to member countries

    • Helps maintain a stable economy

2.3 Positive impacts¶

Multinational corporation

  • MNCs in developed countries carry out regional division of labour (Outsource low-skilled production processes to developing countries)

  • On Developed countries

  • Set up production bases in developing countries with cheaper labour and land

    • -> reduces production costs

    • -> Increase competitiveness of products in international market -> earn more profits

  • Developing high-value-added industries

    • Outsourcing low-skilled production processes -> Focus on developing high value-added industries and achieve economic transformation

  • Market diversification

    • Open up markets in developing countries to achieve market diversification and help diversify risk

  • On developing countries

  • Obtain more advanced technology and capital -> Promote economic growth

  • Employment opportunities and income increase -> Improved living standard for local people

International trade

  • Increased

  • Products are sold all over the world

  • Exporting countries

  • Create a lot of employment opportunities and wealth for the country

  • Focus on the production and export of advantageous commodities and service -> increase total global output of goods and services

  • Importing countries

  • Buy a wider range of imported goods at cheaper prices -> Improve quality of life

International migrant workers

  • Workers are willing to work in other countries

  • MNCs hire employees from overseas -> positive impact on both developed and developing countries

  • Impacts on Developed countries: Ease labour shortage in some industries

  • Impacts on Developing countries:

  • Foreign exchange income can be earned by exporting foreign workers

  • Professionals go to work in developing countries -> New technology, experience and management knowledge

Challenges¶

Production processes are overly interdependent (between countries)

Affecting international trade: One country economy bad -> Affect other countries

Widening global wealth gap: Developed countries focus on high value-added industries -> more profit

Exploiting workers' rights

  • Factories face keen competition -> Offer lower prices to win orders from MNCs

  • Exploit workers' rights to win orders

  • Force workers to work long hours

  • Pay less than legally required

  • May have to work in poor working conditions without adequate safety measures -> sweatshops

Loss of low-skilled jobs

  • Outsource low skilled production processes to developing countries -> low skill workers lose their jobs

Triggering financial crisis: Fluctuations in one market spread to other countries -> Trigger global economic and financial crisis

2.4 Status of HK as a global financial centre¶

Characteristics¶

  • Place where international capital flows

  • Different multinational banks and financial institutions provide financial services

  • Activities: Fund raising, management, investment and other activities are conducted efficiently

Provide different types of financial products

  • Stock market

  • Largest equity fundraising centres

  • Bond market

  • Largest centre for arranging Asian international bond insurance for consecutive years

  • Most important location for arranging initial bond insurances (78% market share)

  • Foreign exchange market

  • Fourth largest foreign exchange market in the world

  • International financial institution

  • Banks

  • Insurance companies

Reasons¶

Favourable time zone

  • International time zone connecting time difference between America and Europe

  • -> filling the gaps between two major financial markets

  • Continue to invest in Hong Kong when the market of two places are closed

  • -> International financial transactions to continue around the clock

Favourable geographical location

  • Southeast tip of China -> Hub of Southeast Asia

  • Close to Mainland market

  • -> facilitates provision of financial services to Asia and Mainland

  • -> Attracts multinational financial corporations

Excellent investment environment

  • Has good

  • legal system

  • financial regulatory system

  • -> Efficient operation of the financial market

  • -> Fair competition

  • X foreign exchange controls, low tax rates, simple tax system -> attract investment activities

Opportunities¶

Capital flow between Mainland and other regions

  • Attracted Mainland enterprises to list in Hong Kong to raise funds

  • -> Mainland's largest overseas fundraising hub

  • CPG launched Stock Connect enchantment measures

  • -> provide HK for channels to Mainland's capital market

  • e.g. Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect, Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock connect, Bond connect

Offshore RMB business hub

  • Highly efficient and robust financial infrastructure with RMB real-time payment and settlement system
  • Convenience for international investors to conduct RMB transactions
  • -> Become a bridge between Mainland China and the international financial system

Attract foreign financial institutions

  • Many multinational financial institutions establish business in HK -> introduce capital and professional knowledge

  • -> Promote the development of financial technology innovation and risk management in HK

  • -> Promote development of HK financial industry

Providing diversified financial services and products

  • More closely connected with investors

  • -> Provide diversified services and products to cater to the needs of international investors

Challenges¶

Financial industry is easily affected by external factors

  • Interdependent

  • Other countries experience economic recession -> HK financial industry will also be affected

  • Needs to be more active in developing diversified business (e.g. asset management, risk management, bond financing)

  • -> increase economic strength

Keen competition among global international financial centres

  • Other may offer more favourable business environments, tax incentives, infrastructure

  • Financial institutions hire from all over the world -> outflow of financial processionals from Hong Kong

  • -> Pose challenges to HK and financial industry

2.5 Role of HK in economic development of our country¶

Bridge connecting the Mainland and the world

  • An international financial, transportation and trade centre

  • Assist Mainland enterprises to identify opportunities in overseas market and expand international business

  • Serve as an bridge for cooperation, trade and investment

Provider of capital, talent and services

  • Make contribution in fields of arbitration, law, architecture, engineering, accounting, financial and insurance services

  • Following the development of the Belt and Road initiative -> Trade volume increased significantly

  • Provide highly efficient logistics services to Hong Kong to promote trade in the region

3 Youth employment¶

3.1 Recent employment situation¶

  • Market demand for labour has undergone changes -> affected the employment of Hong Kong's labour force

Manufacturing industry

  • Factories moved to countries with lower production costs -> decline in number of local industries

  • Development of mechanical automation and artificial intelligence replaces manual work

  • Service industry accounts for a larger proportion (more employment opportunities and more attractive salaries and benefits)

  • -> Lower supply and demand for workers

Financial, professional and other producer services

  • International financial centre with sound legal system

  • Bridge connecting mainland and the world -> attract multinational financial institutions and investors

  • Economy growth of country's economy -> more mainland enterprises are coming to Hong Kong to issue stocks and bonds to raise funds and expand their business

  • -> Higher Demand for services (e.g. Legal, accounting, financing, insurance, investment)

Trading and logistics

  • Port business face keen competition from the Mainland -> trade good no longer re-export through Hong Kong

  • -> Weakening Hong Kong's position as a re-export hub in the region

  • Trade conflicts in 2018 -> impact on trade activities and exports and re-exports of goods

  • -> Lower demand for Hong Kong's trade and logistics services

Tourism

  • Face keen competition from nearby places

  • Hotel prices increase sharply during peak travel seasons -> spend less time staying and sightseeing -> less demand for Hong Kong's tourism services

  • Strict entry restrictions and quarantine in 2018 -> number of tourists decrease

3.2 Opportunities¶

Economic ups and downs¶

  • Highly externally oriented and open economy -> easily affected by external factors

  • -> Poses challenges to youth employment

  • Economy recession -> companies hire fewer workers

  • -> youth may have difficulties getting jobs due to low education level or lack of work experience

Trends of industrial development¶

  • developed into a knowledge economy

  • Develop high-tech and high-value-added industries (e.g. financial technology, information technology)

Innovation and technology (I&T)

  • Promotes re-industrialisation to develop advanced manufacturing based on new tech

  • Enhances HK's High quality development, create high-quality opportunities for young people

Cultural and creative industries

  • 14th 5 year plan supports HK to develop into an East-meets-West centre for international cultural exchange

  • Encourages the development of the arts, culture and creative industries

  • The Policy Address 2022 proposes that government will establish Culture commission

  • -> assist in mapping out a blueprint in Arts and Culture and Creative Industries development

  • Encourages private development projects to include cultural and arts facilities

  • Subsidise cultural and art activities

  • -> provide job opportunities to young people with artistic talent and creativity

Sports industry

  • Promotes sport development in Hong Kong by

  • Promoting community sports

  • Supporting elite sports

  • Developing Hong Kong into a centre for major international sports events

  • -> Promotes development of Hong Kong sports industry

  • -> Attract tourists and increase demand for related support services

  • -> more employment opportunities

3.2 Challenges¶

Low educational levels -> lower incomes

  • Jobs in knowledge economy requires a high level of education

  • May only be able to take up low-skilled jobs and earn lower incomes

Need to grasp multiple skills

  • Constantly learn news skills to meet the needs of job market (Science and technology)

Intensifying competition among people with high education levels

  • More university and college degrees -> Keen competition

Mismatch of job

  • Highly educated may need to accept jobs that don't match their academic qualifications and majors

  • -> Mismatch between academic qualifications and positions

3.2 Future trends¶

Emerging industries¶

Emerging industries

  • AI developing rapidly -> application expands to different fields -> Became increasingly computerised and automated

  • -> Improves the business model and production efficiency

  • Creating new occupations

  • AI technology increasingly mature

  • Can create new products and services and derive new jobs and businesses

  • Would create 130M new jobs and occupations

  • Replacing some types of jobs

  • Low skilled jobs and repetitive jobs will be replaced by AI technology

  • Also assist in analysing complex data and making accurate decisions

  • -> technical tasks (data analysis: accounting, legal consulting, financial analysis) may be replaced by computers

Changes in employment patterns

  • Map not be satisfied with lifestyle of one occupation

  • Gig economy: Freelance workers use websites or applications to sign short form contracts with employers to provide services and earn income

  • e.g. Food deliveries, translators, writers

  • Opportunities

  • More freedom: choose their own working hours, locations, and types -> achieve work-life balance

  • More satisfaction: Work different jobs based on their interests

  • Diverse work experience: Work for different organisations, engage in different types of jobs -> helps develop skills and gain diversified work experience

  • Challenges

  • Unstable income: Do not have a fixed monthly income, may need to work multiple jobs to pay daily expenses

  • Lack of labour protection

    • Do not have a formal employment relationship with companies

    • -> do not provide employee benefits and protections

  • Unfavourable for career development

    • Jobs are low skilled and replaceable

    • May not be favourable for future job hunting

Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area¶

Opportunities

  • Employment opportunities

  • Enterprises have huge demand for highly educated and skilled talents

  • Can choose suitable jobs for their own career development in the region

  • Entrepreneurship opportunities

  • Established Youth Development funds: subsidise Hong Kong non-gov organisations to provide entrepreneurial support services to Hong Kong young people starting their own business

Challenges

  • Difficulty of adaptation

  • Adapt to different living and working cultures

  • Need to have good Putonghua skills to communicate and integrate

  • Keen competition

  • Need to have knowledge, skills and creativity to stand out

  • Lack of understanding

  • Systems are different

  • Lack of understanding of mainland laws and business systems

  • Unfamiliar with region's development planning, policies and industry ecology

  • -> difficult to grasp development opportunities
